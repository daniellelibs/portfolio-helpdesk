## Introduction

This guide covers essential security practices for everyday work. Following these steps protects your data, your organization's network, and prevents common cyber incidents.

---

## 1. Password Security

### Create Strong Passwords

A strong password is your first line of defense against unauthorized access.

**Requirements for a strong password:**
- **Minimum 12 characters** (longer is better)
- **Mix of character types:** uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and special characters (!@#$%^&*)
- **Avoid predictable patterns:** no birthdays, common words, or sequential numbers
- **Unique per account:** never reuse passwords across work and personal accounts

**Example of a strong password:**
❌ `Password123` (too simple, common pattern)  
✅ `Tr0pic@lSunset#2025!` (long, mixed characters, unpredictable)

### Password Management

- **Change passwords every 90 days** (set a calendar reminder)
- **Never share your password** with anyone, including IT staff (legitimate IT will never ask for it)
- **Use a password manager** (Bitwarden, 1Password) to store complex passwords securely
- **Never write passwords on sticky notes** or store them in plain text files

## 2. Recognizing Phishing & Suspicious Emails

Phishing is the #1 way attackers gain access to corporate networks. **If something feels off, it probably is.**

### Red Flags in Emails

| Warning Sign | Example | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Urgent pressure to act | "Your account will be locked in 24 hours!" | Don't click. Verify by calling the sender directly. |
| Requests for personal data | "Confirm your password to unlock your email" | Legitimate companies NEVER ask for passwords via email. |
| Suspicious sender address | `secure-update@goog1e.com` (notice the "1" instead of "l") | Check carefully. Hover over sender name to see real email. |
| Generic greeting | "Dear Valued Customer" or "Dear User" | Real emails use your actual name. |
| Mismatched links | Text says "Click here" but URL shows something different | Hover over links (don't click) to see the real destination. |
| Poor grammar/spelling | Multiple typos or awkward phrasing | Legitimate companies proofread communications. |
| Unexpected attachments | You didn't request a file, but there's an attachment | Don't open. Confirm with sender via phone before downloading. |

### What to Do If You Receive a Phishing Email

1. **Do NOT click any links or download attachments**
2. **Do NOT reply** to the email
3. **Report it** — forward to your IT department or click "Report Phishing" (most email clients have this)
4. **Delete it** after reporting
5. **If you already clicked:** inform IT immediately, and consider a password reset

## 3. Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

MFA adds a second security layer: even if someone steals your password, they still can't access your account without the second factor.

### How MFA Works

1. You enter your password
2. The system sends a code via:
   - **Authenticator app** (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator) — most secure
   - **Text message (SMS)** — acceptable, but less secure
   - **Email code** — least preferred
3. You enter that code to complete login

### Best Practices for MFA

- **Always enable MFA** on accounts that offer it (email, VPN, cloud apps)
- **Save backup codes** in a secure location (password manager, NOT on your desktop)
- **Use authenticator apps over SMS** when possible (SMS can be intercepted)
- **Keep your phone secure** — it's now part of your security

## 4. Social Engineering & Pretexting

**Social engineering** is when someone manipulates you into revealing information or bypassing security, often by pretending to be someone they're not.

### Common Social Engineering Tactics

| Tactic | Example | Protection |
|---|---|---|
| Impersonation | "Hi, I'm from IT, need your login to fix your computer" | Legitimate IT never asks for passwords. Verify by calling IT directly (use a known number). |
| Authority | "I'm your manager's boss, send me the quarterly report ASAP" | Confirm unusual requests through a separate communication channel. |
| Urgency | "The system is down! Everyone needs to reset passwords NOW!" | Take a breath. Real emergencies come with official communications. Verify independently. |
| Building trust | Attacker learns about you, mentions personal details to seem legitimate | Personal details don't equal legitimacy. Stay skeptical. |

### Your Defense

- **Verify before sharing:** if someone asks for sensitive information, hang up and call them back using a known number
- **Trust your instincts:** if it feels wrong, it's wrong
- **When in doubt, ask IT:** better to be cautious than to be compromised

## 5. General Security Habits

### Device Security

- **Lock your screen** whenever you leave your desk (Windows key + L)
- **Shut down or lock** at the end of the day
- **Keep antivirus/Windows Defender enabled** at all times
- **Install security updates** promptly (don't ignore "Update Available" notifications)
- **Use company-provided VPN** when accessing work systems remotely
- **Never leave your laptop unattended** in public places

### Network Security

- **Only use official company WiFi** (not public WiFi at coffee shops for work tasks)
- **Disable file sharing** on your device unless specifically needed
- **Don't click links in messages from unknown senders** on Slack, Teams, email, etc.

### Data Handling

- **Minimize sensitive data** on your personal devices
- **Encrypt sensitive files** before storing them (Windows BitLocker, built-in)
- **Dispose of printed documents** securely (shred, don't throw in trash)
- **Report lost/stolen devices** to IT immediately

## 6. What to Do If You Suspect a Security Incident

**Don't panic. Do this:**

1. **Stop what you're doing** — disconnect from the network if possible
2. **Contact IT immediately:**
   - Call: [IT Support Number]
   - Email: [IT Support Email]
3. **Be specific:** describe what happened (clicked a link, downloaded a file, noticed unusual activity, etc.)
4. **Don't delete evidence:** keep the email, the file, or screenshots of what you saw
5. **Change your password** from a different device (once IT advises it's safe)

**Remember:** reporting quickly is better than staying silent. IT is here to help, not to blame.
